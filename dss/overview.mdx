Discrete Sovereign Settlement (DSS) is NEOXZ's continuous authentication protocol. It eliminates passwords, OTPs, and seed phrases by binding a user to a hardware Secure Enclave and continuously validating three independent reality-anchored vectors: device identity, social graph context, and geographic location.

## What DSS Solves

Interactive authentication (passwords, OTPs, seed phrases) creates an "Interceptable Window" during which credentials can be phished, SIM-swapped, or lost. It also imposes a large ongoing energy cost through global SMS gateways, email verification servers, and retry cycles caused by human error.

DSS removes user-visible credentials entirely and replaces "validate once at login" with lightweight cryptographic handshakes that fire every 3 seconds during an active session.

## Core Properties

- **No interactive credentials.** Users never enter a password, OTP, or seed phrase after initial biometric enrollment.
- **Continuous validation.** A signed handshake fires every 3 seconds during active sessions instead of trusting a session token after login.
- **Three independent vectors.** Device authenticity, contact/social graph, and location must all be consistent with the enrolled user's reality.
- **Composite trust score.** Access permissions adjust dynamically from a weighted score: `Device × 0.40 + Contact × 0.30 + Location × 0.30`.
- **Seed-phrase-free recovery.** Cryptocurrency keys are split via 3-of-5 Shamir Secret Sharing across trusted contacts, a recovery key, and an identity verification service.
- **Energy efficient.** Roughly 92.1% reduction in authentication-related energy compared with traditional password + OTP infrastructure at equivalent security.

## Attack Surface

To compromise a DSS-protected account, an attacker must simultaneously:

1. Physically possess or perfectly clone the user's device including its Secure Enclave hardware.
2. Replicate the user's entire social graph and communication patterns across all contacts.
3. Be in the same geographic location as the user, or spoof GPS while defeating device attestation.

This combination is effectively unachievable, making DSS roughly 25 million times more expensive to attack than a traditional password + OTP system.
