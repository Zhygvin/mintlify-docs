The authentication gateway computes a composite trust score by combining the three independent verification vectors. Access permissions adjust dynamically based on this score, applying different thresholds per operation risk level.

## Formula

```text Composite trust score
CompositeScore = (DeviceScore × 0.40) + (ContactScore × 0.30) + (LocationScore × 0.30)
```

### Weighting Rationale

- **Device (40%).** Strongest cryptographic guarantee: Secure Enclave keys are effectively unextractable.
- **Contact (30%).** Social graph replication requires extensive surveillance and real-world relationship knowledge.
- **Location (30%).** Enforces physical reality constraints that cannot be violated by purely technical attacks.

## Risk-Based Decision Thresholds

| Risk level | Example operations | Required score |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low | Read data, view content | ≥ 0.50 |
| Medium | Post content, moderate transactions | ≥ 0.75 |
| High | Large transfers, account changes, device management | ≥ 0.90 |
| Critical | Cryptocurrency transfers, account deletion | ≥ 0.95 + trusted contact approval |

## Dynamic Threshold Adjustment

Thresholds adjust based on historical behavior:

- **Established user** (6+ months of consistent behavior, average score `0.92`): low-risk threshold relaxed to `0.45`; high-risk thresholds unchanged.
- **New user or device** (first 14 days): elevated thresholds — `0.85` for medium-risk, `0.95` for high-risk.

## Step-Up Authentication

When the composite score falls between thresholds (for example `0.60` on an operation requiring `0.75`), DSS triggers step-up authentication:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Fresh biometric">
    Fresh biometric scan required within 30 seconds.
  </Step>
  <Step title="One-time code (optional)">
    A verification code is sent to registered email/phone. Used only for step-up, never for regular authentication.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Trusted contact approval (critical operations)">
    For critical operations, DSS sends approval requests to the user's designated [trusted contacts](/dss/vector-contact#trusted-contact-verification).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Graceful Degradation

When one vector is temporarily unavailable (e.g., GPS lost indoors, no contact activity during solo work), DSS continues with the remaining vectors and adjusted weighting:

- **Location unavailable:**
  ```text
  CompositeScore = (DeviceScore × 0.60) + (ContactScore × 0.40)
  ```
- **Contact and location both unavailable:** device score must be `1.0` for authentication to succeed; high-risk operations are blocked until additional vectors restore.

<Note>
  Degradation never lifts the impossible-travel or mock-location hard-fail conditions. Those set the [location score](/dss/vector-location) to `0.0` and can never be bypassed by a fallback weighting.
</Note>
