The contact validation vector analyzes the user's social graph and communication patterns to detect anomalies indicating compromise. It leverages the insight that an attacker cannot replicate the user's real-world relationships and communication behaviors even after gaining device access. It contributes 30% of the composite trust score.

## Social Graph Baseline

During initial registration and the first 30 days of use, DSS builds a baseline covering:

- Contact list from the device address book (names, phone numbers, email addresses).
- Communication frequency with each contact (messages sent/received per week).
- Timing patterns (typical hours, weekday vs. weekend).
- Channels used (SMS, email, social platforms).
- Bidirectional relationship validation (contacts who also list the user).
- Contact proximity patterns (Bluetooth proximity, shared location check-ins).

## Real-Time Pattern Context

Each handshake carries compressed communication context from the preceding interval (3 seconds to 1 hour depending on activity):

- Contact identifiers (hashed for privacy) of recent communication.
- Message counts sent/received.
- Communication timestamps.
- Channels used.
- Bluetooth proximity events indicating physical presence near known contacts.

## Anomaly Detection

| Anomaly | Trigger | Score Penalty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No communication anomaly | Zero outgoing communication for 4+ hours while device is active, for a user who typically messages 5-10 contacts daily | -0.3 |
| Unknown contact surge | 70%+ of recent communication is with contacts not in baseline | -0.5 |
| Temporal pattern violation | Communication at unusual hours (e.g., 3 AM for a user who never messages between 2-6 AM) | -0.2 |
| Channel inconsistency | Sudden switch of primary channel (e.g., SMS-only user now email-only) | -0.3 |
| Proximity absence | Zero Bluetooth proximity to expected known contacts during typical shared hours | -0.2 |

## Trusted Contact Verification

For high-risk operations, DSS can invoke trusted contact verification. During setup, the user designates 3-5 trusted contacts. For verification, the system sends a one-tap approval request:

> "User X is attempting to transfer \$50,000 to unknown recipient. Approve?"

This prevents insider threats and provides social recovery without seed phrases.

## Scoring

The contact validation score combines:

- **Communication recency:** `1.0` if messages in last hour, `0.8` if last 4 hours, `0.6` if last 24 hours, `0.3` if 1+ days.
- **Pattern consistency:** `1.0` if normal, reduced by the anomaly penalties above.
- **Social graph familiarity:** `1.0` if 90%+ of communication is with known contacts, `0.5` at 50%, `0.0` below 25%.
- **Proximity verification:** `1.0` if expected proximity matches reality, `0.7` neutral, `0.3` contradictory.

<Note>
  For sessions with zero social activity but normal device and location scores, the contact score defaults to `0.6` (neutral rather than penalizing solo work).
</Note>
